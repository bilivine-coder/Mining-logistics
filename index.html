01   The loop

A fuel statement that doesn't match the trip tickets. A tonnage ticket that never became an invoice. A KPI pack that lands after the month has already closed. None of it is a line item. All of it is money.

At around ten trucks, the manual layer is small enough to carry and large enough to hide things in. GPS, fuel cards, and trip tickets get reconciled against invoices by hand. Tonnage tickets get matched to billing by memory and follow-up. Maintenance, tyres, and parts live in someone's spreadsheet. Nothing fails loudly. It just leaks.

And that's the smaller half.

The hours are what you can see. These are what the manual layer costs while everyone is busy closing the month.

Fuel leakage

Slow reconciliation means a fuel problem is found a month after it started, every month, for as long as it lasts.

Unbilled loads

Hand-matching tonnage tickets to invoices is where loads go missing and where disputes quietly age.

Cash sitting still

Late invoicing and slow disputes leave earned revenue uncollected. The work is done; the money hasn't moved.

Decisions made late

If KPIs only land after month-end, tyre wear, idle trucks and workshop delays keep costing for weeks.

At ten trucks, one percent of fuel spend or one percent of haulage revenue is each larger than the entire labour saving above. That ratio is the argument — not the exact figures.

03   How it works

Four unglamorous gaps. That's the whole build.

01

Reconciliation that runs itself

GPS, fuel cards, trip tickets and supplier invoices match automatically. Exceptions surface the same week — not at close, and not a month after it.

02

Documents that chase themselves

Missing PODs, approvals and supplier paperwork get followed up on a schedule, instead of waiting in an inbox for a quiet afternoon.

03

Invoices out the same week

Tonnage tickets turn into billing as they're matched, so revenue isn't queuing and disputes aren't ageing in a spreadsheet.

04

Reporting in days, not weeks

Fuel, tyre and utilisation KPIs build from the same data the reconciliations use — so decisions don't wait for the close.
